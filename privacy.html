PAC OpenClaw privacy notice

Updated 8 November 2026. At preparation, an OAuth client was stored privately with no Google account grant made through it. This notice describes the initial connection and separately identifies features that remain inactive. It does not certify compliance or claim that all operational safeguards have passed acceptance.

Operator and access

PAC OpenClaw is operated privately for its account owner and individually authorised household users. Account holders contact the operator through the private channel used to arrange their access or request deletion. Do not put private records, credentials or account details in public GitHub issues.

There is no public sign-up. Google's External app status is not a Google-enforced household allowlist. The operator controls local account provisioning and must obtain each account holder's consent. This connection proposal covers the operator's own Google account only. Another household account would require a separate, narrower decision. Work NHSmail is excluded.

What the first connection would do

The account holder signs in with Google through the existing native command-line client's private manual OAuth flow. Google verifies the identity and receives the requested permissions. The client receives refresh/access credentials, checks that the authorised identity matches the intended account, and stores its refresh credentials in the existing private encrypted keyring.

Immediately before starting that private consent flow, the operator must show the account holder a contextual notice stating the exact account and permissions, identity/token-only actions now, inactive content/AI/writes/routines, private storage and withdrawal/deletion limits. The account holder must affirm that notice before the OAuth flow starts. A link to this public page alone is not that contextual confirmation.

This connection step does not authorise reading mailbox contents, events or files; changing Google data; sending messages; activating routines; or sending Google content to an AI service. Those operations remain inactive until their applicable tests and approvals. An OAuth grant provides technical rights broader than the actions permitted during this step.

Requested permissions and intended personal workflows

The proposed native request covers identity and five Google services. It contains some read-only scope names alongside broader rights; the exact reviewed list must be shown before consent. It does not request the dedicated Gmail settings scopes, full Calendar scope or Contacts scopes. Included Gmail and Calendar read permissions can nevertheless allow some settings or known access-rule reads. Operational tools must not access settings or calendar access rules without separate authorisation.

  • Identity: OpenID and email permissions identify and verify the connected account.
  • Gmail: read-only and modify permissions can read complete messages and threads, including relevant sent messages and Spam, change labels/read state, send mail and move mail to Bin. Intended use is personal email assistance, existing narrow read-state rules and separately approved replies. The assistant policy forbids deleting or trashing mail; sending requires approval for the exact action. Broad Gmail rights do not create permission to clear a mailbox.
  • Calendar: read-only calendar access and event-write access support relevant calendar checks and authorised Family-calendar event creation or updates. The event scope is broader than Family alone and can technically modify or delete events in other accessible calendars. Such broader actions are not operationally authorised.
  • Drive: read-only and broad Drive access support existing authorised records, supplied food-label uploads and the documented limited handling of processed activity exports. This OAuth permission is not limited to selected files or folders and can technically change, share or delete other accessible files. Those rights are not granted as general assistant authority.
  • Docs: document read-only access and the already requested broad Drive edit rights support existing knowledge and relocation records and exact approved edits. A separate Docs write scope is not requested. Knowledge changes require the applicable narrow approval and protection against overwriting a changed revision.
  • Sheets: spreadsheet read-only access and the same broad Drive edit rights support existing records and Coach's existing food diary, including authorised additions/corrections. A separate Sheets write scope is not requested. These rights do not authorise replacing whole sheets or mixing different people's records.

The native client already implements these command families. Resource restrictions, approval enforcement, full-message coverage, duplicate-safe logging and recovery must still be tested for the new assistant before activation. Prompt rules and OAuth consent alone do not enforce those restrictions. Granting rights now solely for an unimplemented future feature would not be justified; the operator must establish the current feature purpose before requesting consent. Any material change in purposes or permissions requires an updated disclosure and applicable consent.

Data used when later features are activated

Authorised features may process full email bodies and threads, event details, selected Drive files, document text and spreadsheet ranges. These can include sensitive personal information. PAC coordinates personal administration; Coach handles the authorised food/training records; Roo handles relocation records. Relevant information may be shared between these assistants only for the approved task. Buzz remains paused.

No Google content is currently flowing through this client's connection to an AI model. Before that changes, the operator must test and approve the actual resource, transfer and model route. Selected content needed for a request could then be sent to OpenAI for the user-facing assistant response. Approved assistant replies could include relevant details in a private Telegram bot chat; those chats are not end-to-end encrypted. Google connection consent alone does not activate either transfer.

AI-provider controls and limits

The planned model routes are the OpenAI API and a separately authenticated personal ChatGPT/Codex route. The API project's input/output training sharing is excluded. The personal ChatGPT training setting was observed off on 8 November 2026; OpenAI documents its application to new Codex tasks. A separate environment-sharing control and feedback exceptions are distinct, and their full applicability to the native route is not established. Google content must not be submitted through feedback or an unreviewed environment-sharing path.

The operator does not authorise Google data for advertising, resale or general-model training. Training opt-out does not mean zero retention or no human access. OpenAI may retain data and allow authorised personnel/service-provider access for security, support or legal reasons; processing can occur in the US and elsewhere. Exact retention, environment-sharing and deletion coverage for this native assistant route remain unresolved. No Google-to-AI flow will be activated on the assumption that these points are settled.

See OpenAI training controls, consumer data handling and API data controls. API controls must not be treated as proof for a ChatGPT-authenticated route.

Private storage and human access

The private assistant is hosted on an OVHcloud VPS. The existing server OAuth client is held in a native encrypted file keyring under a dedicated account, with its unlock material kept separately from the ordinary working data. Future refresh credentials would use that keyring. The original downloaded client-credential file is also retained privately; server encryption does not erase that copy.

Private migration exports, candidate memories and test records already exist from authorised preparation. Future approved operation may retain relevant excerpts, working state, task/food records, outputs and diagnostic metadata. Keyring encryption does not establish encryption of every export, memory file, log or backup. Agents must not receive arbitrary credential-bearing shell access. Human inspection of Google-derived messages, files or other data requires the account holder's affirmative agreement to the specific items being inspected, unless a recognised security or legal exception under Google's policy applies. A general troubleshooting or administrator permission is not sufficient. The system is not protected against trusted-root compromise.

Retention, withdrawal and deletion

Existing private exports, candidate memories and test files are retained until owner-directed review or deletion; no automatic system-wide deletion period is configured. Later operational retention must be agreed and implemented before the relevant feature is activated. Complete independent backup/restore and deletion coverage have not been established. Do not assume that deleting one file removes working copies, snapshots, backups or provider records.

Account holders can revoke Google access in Google Account connections. Revocation stops further access under that grant; it does not itself remove local credentials, previous exports, memories, test outputs or provider-held records. Ask the operator privately to remove the relevant local connection/copies and to report what was removed, retained or could not be verified. No instant or universal erasure guarantee is made.

OpenAI's Codex chat deletion guidance describes deletion timing and exceptions for its account chats. Whether every native assistant record is represented there, and the deletion mechanism for all such records, is not established. Its stated periods must not be presented as this application's proven end-to-end deletion deadline.
